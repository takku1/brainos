# 22 - Capability Discovery and Organizational Planning

Discovery diagnoses a demand and chooses a bounded response: reuse existing
organization, propose a transformation/sequence, acquire discriminating
information, or abstain. Doc 18 owns demand records; Doc 19 owns outcome
models; Doc 27 owns executable construction; Doc 32 owns execution.

## 22.1 Provider contract

Inputs: demand/goal, permitted registry/impact projection, authorized
transformation history, current constraints, and a probe/search grant.
Outputs: ranked nominations or short plans/probes with predicted outcomes,
support, guards and costs; an existing-solution finding; or inconclusive.
Strategies may be handwritten planners, memoized search, case retrieval,
learned models, or portfolios. No strategy can publish, authorize itself,
access hidden provider state, or inspect final evaluation data.

## 22.2 Decision procedure

1. Characterize residual failure, cost, uncertainty, or backlog by context,
   affected contract, measurement window, and grounded support.
2. Decompose the goal into sub-demands and test existing capabilities first.
   A prerequisite can be worth acquiring before terminal reward appears.
3. Retrieve applicable successful/failed transformations. Distinguish exact
   supported reuse from a broader matching hypothesis (Doc 19 section 19.5).
4. Generate a bounded set of changes/sequences using Doc 24 contracts and
   Doc 27's declared grammar. Reuse/configure/compose checks prioritize cheap
   alternatives, without forbidding a necessary enabling construction step.
5. Discriminate information, representation, algorithm and coordination needs
   with matched bounded probes. Separate inferred applicability from type-check
   facts and formally verified constraints.
6. Predict outcome vectors using an explicit strategy/version; check guards,
   uncertainty, prefix constraints and total budget. Compare at equal horizon;
   a myopic policy is not a substitute for competent planning.
7. Emit a nominated contract/evaluation plan for each acquisition, or a probe
   with question, possible outcomes, budget and stopping rule. Record predictions
   before trials, including the outcome expected from no change.
8. Execute only the next admitted step, measure the outcome, and replan. Later
   steps are proposals conditional on actual results, not preauthorized work.

## 22.3 Stopping and funding

Doc 20 separates uncertainty-reduction probes, evidence-supported construction,
and bounded enabling-step grants. Initially depth <= 2 with fixed branching,
candidate, trial, time and resource caps. End with a nomination/plan, existing
solution, no-change, or an inconclusive record naming missing evidence.
Exhaustion and rejected paths retain costs/outcome status. A promising model
score neither enlarges a grant nor justifies indefinite repeated probes.

## 22.4 Interaction-aware diagnosis

For capability-induced demand, probe artifact removal/freezing, collaborator
substitution, provider prevalence, and previous resource allocations when
admissible. Pin versions; record assignment, context, interference and
inconclusive conditions. Invalid combinations are rejected, not misclassified
as executed poor performance. Distinguish complementarity, contention,
representation mismatch, new feasibility, and a provider defect.

Challenge generators propose contexts subject to independent validity and
relevance checks. Model-guided discovery cannot rewrite its acceptance test.
Doc 25 owns ecological mechanisms; Doc 07 sections 7.10-7.12 separate
interaction, expansion, and learned organizational planning experiments.
